Restaurants and Specialities of the North

Lille's food culture centres on estaminets — small, old-fashioned Flemish restaurants serving generous regional dishes alongside a solid regional beer list. Here's what to order, and where.

What to try

  • Carbonnade flamande: beef slow-cooked in dark beer
  • Welsh: a slice of bread, ham and melted cheddar (or maroilles, depending on the address), usually served with fries
  • Potjevleesch: a cold terrine of several meats set in jelly
  • Poulet au maroilles: chicken in a creamy sauce made with the region's pungent cheese
  • Meert waffle: Madagascar vanilla, a Lille institution since 1761

Where to go

Au Vieux de la Vieille

2 rue des Vieux Murs, Place aux Oignons

The city's best-known estaminet, in a warm setting on one of Vieux-Lille's prettiest squares. All the regional classics are here, alongside a good twenty beers on the menu.

La Ch'tite Brigitte

10 rue des Bouchers

A small, no-frills, rustic address known for its maroilles gratin. Booking ahead is strongly advised — the place is tiny and popular.

Les Ptiots

35 rue de Gand

Traditional, home-style cooking on rue de Gand, a district slightly less touristy than the heart of Vieux-Lille and often more affordable.

Le Barbier qui fume

69 rue de la Monnaie

Industrial red-brick decor for wood-fired cooking using local produce — a more contemporary alternative to the classic estaminet.

Les Compagnons de la Grappe

26 rue Lepelletier

A cobbled courtyard hidden a few steps from the Grand'Place, with a terrace you'd never guess at from the street. Northern cooking and a long wine list. You come here for the setting above all — book ahead in good weather, the terrace fills fast.

Tripletta

43 rue de Gand · Italian

For anyone wanting a break from northern specialities. The pizzas are excellent and the room is warm — brick, wood, salvaged light fittings. Worth knowing before you go: it isn't the cheapest address on the street.

Oasis Lille Citadelle

1 avenue Mathias Delobel · Citadelle park

At the entrance to the park, facing the Deûle and shaded by trees. More of a garden café than a restaurant: come for a simple lunch, or just a drink after a walk. The terrace is the real draw, and it's taken by storm at the first hint of sun.

Practical tips

Booking
Advisable at weekends for Vieux-Lille addresses, which fill up fast
Budget
Rue de Gand tends to be gentler on the wallet than the heart of Vieux-Lille
To take away
A Meert waffle travels well as an edible souvenir
