Lille's Christmas market: the 2026 guide

Christmas Village on Place Rihour, Ferris wheel on the Grand'Place · [DATES TO CONFIRM]

This is the busiest time of year in Lille, and not only with tourists: locals go too, often more than once. The Christmas Village takes over Place Rihour for six weeks, the Ferris wheel goes up on the Grand'Place, and the whole centre switches to festive lighting.

Here's what you need to know to enjoy it without fighting the crowds.

Where it actually happens

Two spots, two minutes apart on foot:

  • Place Rihour — the Christmas Village itself, around ninety wooden chalets. This is where the traders, the craftspeople and the food are.
  • Grand'Place — the Ferris wheel and the big tree. This is the backdrop, not the market.

The two squares almost touch. Come out of Rihour metro station and you're at the foot of the chalets.

The Ferris wheel

Fifty metres high, thirty-six cabins. It has been part of the Lille skyline since the early 1990s, and the view over the lit rooftops is worth the ticket — especially at dusk, when the lights below start to outshine the sky.

My advice: ride it on a weeknight. On a Saturday the queue can easily take forty minutes for a ride that lasts a few.

What's on the stalls

A mix of crafts and northern produce. In practice:

  • Regional food — maroilles cheese, local beers, Flanders honey
  • Decorations and wooden toys made by Hauts-de-France craftspeople
  • Things to eat standing up: waffles, mulled wine, churros

As at any Christmas market, quality varies from chalet to chalet. The genuine local makers are easy to spot: they're the ones who made what they're selling, and it shows in how they talk about it.

When to go

This is the question that changes everything. The market is the same at any hour; the experience isn't.

  • Best time — a Tuesday or Wednesday evening, around 6pm. It's dark, the lights are on, and you can actually move.
  • Avoid — Saturday afternoon, especially the two weekends before Christmas. You shuffle down the aisles.
  • Sunday morning works well with children: quiet, though you lose the lit-up atmosphere.

Getting there

By metro, Rihour station puts you on the square. From Lille-Flandres station it's a five-minute walk down rue Faidherbe — and you arrive via the Grand'Place, past the Ferris wheel, which is the nicer approach.

By car, forget the centre. Streets around the Grand'Place close to traffic at weekends and the underground car parks fill fast. Use a park-and-ride on the outskirts and take the metro: quicker and far cheaper.

What it costs

Entry to the village is free. The spending comes from everything else: a few euros for a waffle or a mulled wine, and around eight euros for a turn on the Ferris wheel. Exact prices change yearly — check on site.

If you want more

The north has plenty of Christmas markets, and two are worth the trip from Lille:

  • Roubaix — about fifty chalets around the town hall, weighted towards local designers. Twenty minutes by metro.
  • Valenciennes — a medieval-themed village with street performances. Around forty minutes by train.
