Lille's funfair: the complete guide

Events · Two editions a year, in spring and late summer

This is the fixture every Lille resident has known since childhood, and yet it rarely appears in guidebooks. Twice a year the Champ de Mars esplanade turns into the largest funfair in northern France: rides, white-knuckle attractions, shooting galleries, and the smell of croustillons and candy floss across several hectares.

It isn't an attraction designed for tourists. It's a popular tradition, and that's exactly what makes it worth seeing if you want the city as it actually lives.

When it happens

Two editions a year, on the same site:

  • The spring edition, usually from April to early May. In 2026 it ran from 4 April to 3 May, covering the Easter holidays.
  • The late-summer edition, from late August to late September. In 2026: 29 August to 27 September.

Dates shift slightly each year and are confirmed by the city a few weeks ahead. If you're planning a trip around the fair, check lille.fr rather than relying on last year's dates.

Worth knowing: the late-summer edition always overlaps the Braderie weekend. Come for one and you get the other.

Where it is

On the Champ de Mars esplanade, on the edge of the Citadelle, in the Vauban district. It's a vast open space, so the fair spreads out without the crushed feeling you get at city-centre funfairs.

What you'll find

The classic mix, but on a large scale:

  • Rides for small children: carousels, mini bumper cars, caterpillar trains
  • Thrill rides for teenagers and adults
  • A Ferris wheel, shooting galleries, hook-a-duck
  • And above all the fairground food: croustillons, sugared peanuts, candy floss, toffee apples

The showmen change partly between editions, so even if you came in spring you won't find exactly the same fair in late summer.

What to budget

Getting in is completely free. You pay for the rides, and that's where the money goes fast with a family. The simplest approach is to set an amount in advance rather than paying ride by ride.

Some days carry buy-one-get-one-free offers. These vary by edition and are announced on the fair's official Facebook page. There are cash machines on site, but bringing cash saves you the queue.

When to go

Local tip

Come in the late afternoon, around 6pm. The lights come on and the fair becomes something else entirely. In the middle of a sunny afternoon it loses much of its charm.

  • On a weekday if you're coming with children: far shorter queues and a calmer atmosphere.
  • During the Braderie weekend the fair stays open well into the night. It's the liveliest moment of the year, and also the busiest.

Opening hours vary by day and by school holidays, with later closing on Fridays and Saturdays. Rides may also stay shut in strong wind or heavy rain.

Getting there

By metro, two options: République – Beaux-Arts on line 1, then a fifteen-minute walk through the Vauban district; or Port de Lille on line 2, which is slightly closer.

By car, two paid car parks sit either side of the fair, Petit Paradis and Liberté, for around 750 spaces in total. They fill up fast at weekends and in the evening. If you must drive, aim for early afternoon on a weekday, or park further out and walk the last stretch.

Free toilets are provided by the city on site during opening hours.
