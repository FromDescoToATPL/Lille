Lille Street Food Festival: the guide

Events · Every year in early October · EuraTechnologies

This is the big food event of the Lille autumn. Four days, around fifty stalls, local chefs and cuisines from all over, gathered in a red-brick textile mill turned digital campus. Entry is free and the mood is as much festival as market: live music, workshops, DJ sets and an art trail between the stands.

When it happens

Every year in early October, Thursday to Sunday. The 2026 edition is the sixth and runs from 1 to 4 October.

The hours follow a simple pattern: evenings on Thursday and Friday, full days on Saturday and Sunday. In 2026 it opens at 6pm on the first two days, then around 11.30am at the weekend, closing late on Saturday night and in the late afternoon on Sunday. The full programme is published by the organisers in early September.

The venue

The festival takes over EuraTechnologies, place de Saintignon, 165 avenue de Bretagne, in the Bois-Blancs district. This is the former Leblan-Lafont spinning mill, a nineteenth-century textile factory that has become France's leading start-up incubator.

The choice matters. The large industrial halls let the festival run indoors as well as out, which settles the weather question. In the north in early October, that counts.

What you'll eat, and what it costs

Around fifty stalls, with plenty of room given to local restaurants alongside kitchens from further afield. You'll find reinvented chip shops next to bao buns, wood-fired pizza and wok-cooked Asian dishes.

Reckon on €5 to €12 a dish. The idea is to taste several small things rather than sit down to a full meal at one stall.

One important point: the festival is entirely cashless. You pay with a prepaid card topped up on site or online beforehand. Plan for it, because the top-up queue can be long at peak times. And remember to claim back any unused balance before you leave.

When to go to avoid the crowds

Local tip

Come on the Thursday evening. It's by far the quietest time of the festival, and the best if you want to eat in peace without queuing at every stall.

  • Saturday between noon and 3pm is the absolute peak: long queues at the popular stalls and nowhere to sit.
  • Sunday late afternoon is pleasant: still busy, but the crowd thins out and some stalls discount their last portions.

Getting there

By metro, line 2 to Canteleu or Bois Blancs, then a few minutes on foot. It's the simplest option and the only one that spares you the parking question.

By car, allow a quarter of an hour from the centre. A partner car park on rue des Templiers offers a reduced day rate, and a temporary car park is set up on site. Both fill quickly on the Saturday.

Worth knowing

  • Entry is free, with no ticket or booking.
  • Animals are not admitted, except guide and assistance dogs.
  • The organisers run a dedicated welcome for visitors with disabilities, with volunteers at the entrance.
  • Part of the site is outdoors: bring a jacket, it's early October in Flanders.
