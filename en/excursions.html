Day Trips from Lille

Lille sits right on the Belgian border, and its high-speed train station makes it a genuinely practical base for day trips without changing hotels.

Four cities you can do in a day

Brussels

About 35 minutes direct (Thalys, TGV, Eurostar)

The fastest and most frequent connection of the three, with around fifteen direct trains a day. Easily enough for a full day in the Belgian capital (Grand-Place, the European quarter, beer bars) and still be back in Lille to sleep.

Tournai (Belgium)

About 30 minutes by regional train

The closest Belgian city, often skipped by visitors in a hurry. Its belfry — the oldest in Belgium, UNESCO-listed — and its five-towered Notre-Dame cathedral can be seen in half a day, in a noticeably less touristy atmosphere than Bruges or Brussels.

Bruges

About 1h15–1h30, with one change (usually at Kortrijk)

The furthest of the three but the most spectacular, for its canals and intact medieval centre. Since the journey involves a connection, it's worth setting off early and giving it a full day rather than a half-day.

Ghent (Belgium)

About 55 minutes by coach, or 1h15 by direct train

Often overshadowed by Bruges, Ghent is the one I'd recommend first. The same canals and stepped gables, but a real university city that lives all year round rather than a set built for visitors. The Graslei and Korenlei quays in the late afternoon, the Castle of the Counts, and terraces full of locals.

The trick is to take the coach. FlixBus runs Lille to Ghent in about 55 minutes for a few euros, leaving from boulevard de Turin near Lille-Europe. That's quicker than the train, which takes 1h15. The coach drops you at Gent Dampoort, a kilometre from the centre — about fifteen minutes on foot, or two tram stops.

By train, you leave Lille-Flandres for Gent-Sint-Pieters, direct and roughly hourly. More comfortable, more expensive, and the station is further from the centre.
