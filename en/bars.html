Bars and Nightlife in Lille

Three neighbourhoods, three very different moods. Pick depending on what kind of evening you're after.

Where to go, neighbourhood by neighbourhood

Rue Solférino — the student strip

Solférino / Masséna district

Lille's liveliest street after dark, packed with bars running long happy hours and cheap pints. It's loud, festive, and clearly aimed at a student crowd. Le Solférino (156 rue Solférino) is a welcoming bar-tabac with a small terrace and pub games; a little further along, Seven Bar (124 rue Solférino) is another neighbourhood regular.

Vieux-Lille — for a quieter drink

Historic centre

A noticeably calmer mood, with wine bars like Au Gré du Vin or Nectar Dégustation, where the point is discovering a good bottle rather than working through a round of shots. The stone-and-exposed-beam setting suits an evening well.

Wazemmes — the most eclectic district

Around place Sébastopol and the market

A mix of craft beer bars, cocktail spots and more alternative addresses, in a lively, multicultural neighbourhood. The mood shifts a lot from one street to the next, which makes it a good area for bar-hopping without a fixed plan.

Central Lille — for sport and big groups

Around the Grand'Place and the Opera

The centre draws a wider crowd than the other districts: students, people out after work, groups of friends and a lot of visitors from abroad. This is where the big venues are — the ones that can absorb fifty people without becoming unbearable.

Delirium Café, 14 rue Léon Trulin, is the clearest example. Spread over two floors with four separate serving counters, it avoids the usual bottleneck of a packed bar: you never wait twenty minutes for a pint on a Saturday night. The beer list is long, mostly Belgian, and a pint runs between €6 and €8 depending on what you pick. Weekday happy hour runs from 4pm to 7pm: turn up early and the bill looks very different. Times can shift from season to season — the board at the entrance is what counts.

Above all it's the address to know if you want to watch a match. There are screens everywhere, the week's fixtures are posted up, and big football or rugby nights fill the place. You'll regularly find supporters who've travelled from abroad to follow their team: if you're in Lille on a European night, you'll find people cheering the same shirt as you.

One word of warning: on big match nights the room fills well before kick-off. Arrive an hour early if you want a seat.
